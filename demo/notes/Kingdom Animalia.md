# Kingdom Animalia

The kingdom Animalia comprises multicellular, eukaryotic organisms that are typically motile,
heterotrophic (they consume other organisms for energy), and lack cell walls. It is one of several
kingdoms within the domain Eukarya, as introduced in the [[Taxonomy Overview]].

Animalia is enormously diverse, containing more than a million described species, from sponges and
jellyfish to insects, fish, birds, and mammals. Zoologists divide the kingdom into roughly 30 phyla
based on body plan, developmental patterns, and genetic lineage. Chordata, the phylum containing all
vertebrates, is one of the most studied.

Within Chordata, the class Mammalia includes warm-blooded animals with fur or hair that nurse their
young. Carnivorous mammals belong to the order Carnivora, which includes the family Felidae — the
cats. One notable genus in this family is [[Genus Panthera]], home to the world's largest cats.

Classifying animals this way lets scientists compare traits across species, trace evolutionary
relationships, and predict how organisms might respond to environmental change.
