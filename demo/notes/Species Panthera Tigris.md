# Species: Panthera tigris (Tiger)

The tiger, Panthera tigris, is the largest living cat species and a member of the genus
[[Genus Panthera]]. Following the rules of [[Binomial Nomenclature]], its scientific name combines
the genus "Panthera" with the species epithet "tigris."

Tigers are native to Asia, with populations historically ranging from the Russian Far East to
Southeast Asia and the Indian subcontinent. They are easily recognized by their orange coat with
black vertical stripes, a pattern unique to each individual, much like a human fingerprint.

Unlike lions, tigers are solitary and territorial, marking large home ranges and hunting alone,
primarily at dawn and dusk. Their diet consists mainly of large ungulates such as deer and wild boar.

Several subspecies are recognized, including the Bengal, Siberian, and Sumatran tiger, each adapted
to different climates and habitats. Tigers are classified as endangered, with wild populations
reduced by habitat fragmentation and poaching — a decline that has made them one of the most
prominent symbols of global wildlife conservation.
