# Genus Panthera

Panthera is a genus within the family Felidae, part of the kingdom [[Kingdom Animalia]]. It contains
the world's largest big cats, distinguished from other felines mainly by a specialized larynx
structure that allows them to roar rather than purr continuously.

The genus includes five living species: the lion (Panthera leo), the tiger
([[Species Panthera Tigris]]), the jaguar (Panthera onca), the leopard (Panthera pardus), and the
snow leopard (Panthera uncia). Fossil evidence suggests Panthera diverged from other cat lineages
roughly 6 to 10 million years ago, making it one of the older branches of the cat family tree.

Panthera species are apex predators found across a wide range of habitats, from African savannas to
Asian rainforests and mountain ranges. Most are solitary hunters, with lions being a notable exception
due to their social pride structure.

Several Panthera species are threatened by habitat loss and poaching, making them a frequent focus of
international conservation efforts and a key indicator of ecosystem health in the regions they
inhabit.
