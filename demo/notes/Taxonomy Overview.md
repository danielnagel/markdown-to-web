# Taxonomy Overview

Biological taxonomy is the science of naming, defining, and classifying groups of living organisms
based on shared characteristics. The system is hierarchical, moving from broad categories to
increasingly specific ones: Domain, Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, and Species.

Modern taxonomy builds on the work of Carl Linnaeus, who introduced [[Binomial Nomenclature]] in the
18th century — a system still in use today for naming species.

At the broadest level, all life is divided into three domains: Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya. Within
Eukarya sits [[Kingdom Animalia]], the animal kingdom, itself split into phyla, classes, orders,
families, and genera such as [[Genus Panthera]].

Each rank groups organisms by shared ancestry and traits, so a species like
[[Species Panthera Tigris]] belongs to a specific genus, family, order, and so on, all the way up to
its domain.

Taxonomy is not static — classifications are revised as new genetic and fossil evidence reshapes our
understanding of how species relate to one another.
